Local Quench runs on your computer. Your code, chats and keys stay there. This page explains the few cases where personal data does leave it: visiting this website, the app's update checks and web search, and chats you decide to share with us.

## 2. This website

**Server logs.** When you open a page, the hosting provider's server records your IP address, the time, the page requested, the browser's user agent and the referring page. We use these logs only to keep the site running and secure (for example to find attacks or errors). Legal basis: our legitimate interest in a working, secure website (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). The hosting provider keeps the logs for a limited time under its own rules (14 days at the time of writing) and then deletes them.

**Cookies and similar.** This website sets no cookies and stores nothing in your browser (no local storage, no tracking pixels, no third-party analytics, no advertising; our own counting is described below). That is why it shows no cookie banner: there is nothing to consent to. Fonts are served from this server, not from Google or another font service. The model advisor runs entirely in your browser; what you type into it is not sent anywhere.

**Counting visits and downloads.** To know how the website is used, it keeps daily totals, and nothing else:

- how often each page is opened, and how many visits start on each page;
- where visits come from: only the domain of the site that linked here (for example github.com), or "direct";
- how long a page stays visible, in ranges (under 10 seconds, 10 to 30 seconds, and so on up to over 10 minutes);
- for each visit, the browser's language (for example "pl"), whether it is a phone or a computer, and the operating system, as the browser itself states them in every request;
- how often each download button is clicked, by system and version;
- how often the app checks for updates, by kind of install and version (section 3).

A small script on each page sends these counts to our website, where they are added to the day's totals. Nothing is stored in your browser (no cookies, no local storage), and no IP address, identifier or browser fingerprint is stored or used, so a number cannot be linked to you or to your other visits. In your browser, the script only reads which site you came from and measures how long the page is visible. A browser that sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control sends nothing, and robots are not counted; you can also block the script or JavaScript. We use the totals only to understand how the website and the app are used. As far as any of it were personal data, the legal basis is our legitimate interest in knowing how our website is used (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). We keep the totals as long as they are useful. We also read how often GitHub has served each release file, a number GitHub shows for every release.

**E-mail.** If you write to us, we use your address and message to answer and keep the correspondence for as long as needed to handle the matter and possible claims (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

## 3. The Local Quench app

The app processes your projects, chats and settings on your computer. We do not receive them and are not the controller of that data. The app connects to the internet only in these cases:

- **Update checks.** The app asks whether a newer version exists: first our website (localquench.com, whose hosting logs are described in section 2), then GitHub (GitHub, Inc.), and downloads it from GitHub. The same check also fetches the public list of models for the model advisor. GitHub sees your IP address, as with any visit to github.com, under [its own privacy statement](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement). The check tells our website the app's version and kind of install (for example Windows or AppImage), so that we can count update checks per day; nothing that identifies your computer is sent. You can turn automatic checks off in Settings; then neither happens.
- **Price list for cloud models.** If you have set up a cloud provider, the app fetches current prices from OpenRouter (OpenRouter, Inc., United States) to show what a chat would cost there. With local models only, it does not.
- **Web search and reading web pages,** only when the agent uses those tools. Searches go to DuckDuckGo, pages are fetched directly from the site in question. Turn the tools off in Settings if you do not want this.
- **Cloud models you connect yourself** (for example Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, OpenRouter, NVIDIA). Your prompts and code then go to that provider under your own account and its terms. We are not part of that relationship and see nothing of it.
- **Phone access** works in one of two ways you choose. Over Tailscale between your own devices: no server of ours is involved, and Tailscale's own privacy policy covers its coordination service. Or directly (WebRTC): the phone and the computer connect to each other over the internet, encrypted end to end. To find each other they leave a few short messages in a mailbox on our server; these are encrypted with a key only your two devices have, so we cannot read them, and they are deleted when picked up or after 5 minutes at most. To keep the mailbox from being flooded, the server counts requests per address for a few minutes, using only a scrambled form of the IP address that changes daily; nothing is logged. To learn their public address, the devices ask public STUN servers (by default Google's and Cloudflare's), which see the IP address, as for any internet connection. Your chats, code and files never pass through our server.
- **Agent bridge (optional, off by default).** If you turn on the agent bridge in Local Quench and connect another program on your computer to it, such as Claude Code, Codex or Claude Desktop, that program can start tasks in Local Quench. The text of each task, its result, the list of changed files and, if that program asks for it, the conversation of that task go to that program, which may send them to its provider (for example Anthropic or OpenAI). What that program and its provider do with them is governed by their terms, not by this policy, and we are not responsible for it. Inside Local Quench such a task is an ordinary conversation: if you have turned on sharing, it is shared with us like any other conversation, under the consents you gave. It is marked with the name of the program that started it, and since its instructions were written by a cloud model, it is never used to train models, like a chat with a cloud model. If you do not want this, leave the bridge off.
- **Chat sharing,** only if you turn it on. See the next section.

GitHub, OpenRouter, DuckDuckGo, Google and Cloudflare (STUN) are companies based in the United States and see your IP address under their own policies, as with any connection to them. Where such a company is certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, that is the basis for the transfer; otherwise the standard contractual clauses in its terms.

## 4. Sharing chats to improve Local Quench

**What.** If you turn sharing on, the app sends us your chats with the agent from that moment on (and earlier ones only if you also tick "Also chats from before I turned it on"): your messages, the model's replies, the tool calls and their output, the model name, harness settings and timings. Before anything leaves your computer, the app removes API keys, tokens, passwords, e-mail addresses, your home folder and the beginning of every other absolute path, and you can see the original next to the redacted version in Settings. The redaction is pattern matching and cannot recognise everything: a key split in two or encoded, names, company details or personal data inside your code may remain. Do not turn sharing on for projects with personal data of other people or confidential code.

**Identity.** Chats are stored under a random install identifier created by the app. We do not ask for your name or e-mail, and we use your IP address only to deliver the upload and, to keep the server from being flooded, to count requests per address for a few minutes in a scrambled form that changes daily; not to identify you. Because a chat can still contain personal data, we treat shared chats as personal data.

**Purpose.** Two purposes, each with its own consent. (a) Improving Local Quench: analysing where models and the harness fail, evaluating changes and building test tasks. (b) Training and evaluating machine learning models, only if you also tick "Also allow training models with my shared chats". Models trained this way may be published (for example as downloadable weights); the chats themselves are not. Without consent (b) your chats are never used to train a model. Chats with a cloud model (for example Claude, GPT or Gemini) are never used to train models, even with consent (b): their providers' terms do not allow it; the app marks them. We do not sell the data and do not give it to other companies for their own purposes. The full sharing terms are shown in the app before you can turn sharing on.

**Legal basis.** Your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR), given in the app separately for each purpose. Each change (on, off, earlier chats included or not, training on or off, the benchmark setting, each benchmark run you share or withdraw, deletion) is recorded with its time and the version of these terms, on your computer and on our server, so both sides can show what was allowed when.

**Withdrawal and deletion.** You can turn sharing off, or only the permission to train models, at any time in Settings, Data sharing. Without sharing, nothing more is sent: chats and benchmark results that were still waiting to be sent, with their conversations, are dropped, and an upload that is under way at that moment is stopped (what the server had already received by then is covered by deletion). The same holds when you turn off sharing of benchmark results, and for a run you delete in the app before its result was sent; without the training permission, none of your shared chats, earlier ones included, is used in any training that starts afterwards. Withdrawal does not affect what was done lawfully before it. "Delete my shared data" removes everything stored under your install identifier on our server with the next connection, normally within minutes, and in any case within 30 days. The app shows the deletion as done only once our server has confirmed it, with the server's time. Copies of the server's data in our backups are not erased by this: they disappear when those backups are replaced in their normal rotation (the newest 14 daily copies are kept, so deleted data normally leaves them within about two weeks), and are not used for anything else in the meantime. A model that has already been trained on your chats cannot have them removed from it; after deletion your chats are not used for any further training.

**How long.** Shared chats are deleted automatically three years after they were stored, and earlier when you delete them or withdraw consent and ask for deletion; we may delete chats we no longer need before that. The consent record is kept for as long as we need to prove consent, at most 6 years after the last change: after "Delete my shared data" it is moved to a separate archive together with the deletion request, and the install identifier and time of the deletion are noted in a deletion log kept for the same period, so we can show what was deleted and when. A technical change log on the server notes which records were stored, replaced or deleted and when, with your install identifier only in hashed form and without any content; it is kept for the same period. We keep these three records after a deletion on the basis of our legitimate interest in being able to show that consent was given and that data was deleted, and in establishing, exercising or defending legal claims (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR); the 6 years match the general limitation period for claims under Polish law. Once that period has passed, these records are deleted automatically: the server checks for them every day. If a backup ever has to be restored, before it is used we delete from it in the same way the records whose period has passed and everything removed with "Delete my shared data" after the backup was made.

**Where.** On a server we rent from Hetzner Online GmbH (Industriestr. 25, 91710 Gunzenhausen, Germany) in the European Union, which processes the data only on our instructions under a data processing agreement. We do not transfer shared chats outside the European Economic Area. If that changes for training on rented computers, it will happen only under the safeguards of Chapter V GDPR, and this policy will say so before it applies.

### Benchmark results

Separately from chats, you can add a QuenchBench result to the public leaderboard. The app asks after every finished run and shows exactly what would be sent: the model and its quantization, the model server and its settings (engine and version, KV cache type, sampling settings), your processor, graphics card, memory, the benchmark settings (including any harness switches and the label you gave the run), the app version, when the run started and finished, and the per-task results (pass or fail, time, tokens, speed). No code, chats, file paths or commands. Each result is sent only after you click Share for that run. **Conversations of a run.** In the same dialog you can also tick "Also send the agent's conversations from this run" (if chat sharing is on, they always go with a result you share, since you have already allowed sharing chats): then the run's chats go with the result, that is the task text, the model's replies and the commands it ran on our task files, scrubbed like any shared chat (keys, tokens and your home folder removed). QuenchBench tasks are ours, so these conversations contain nothing from your projects. They are stored with the result under your install identifier, used to improve the harness, used to train and evaluate models only if you also gave the separate permission "Also allow training models with my shared chats" (and never when the model was a cloud API; ticking this box alone is not that permission), never sold, and deleted together with the result, at the latest three years after they were stored. A result and its conversations that were still waiting to be sent are not sent when you turn sharing or benchmark sharing off, or delete the run in the app. Legal basis: your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR). Results are stored under your install's random id, published on the leaderboard grouped by model, quantization, KV cache type, graphics card and task selection (a group can be one computer's results), with memory shown only as a range; a run on a cloud API is marked as such and shows no graphics card, and removed with "Delete my shared data".

**Selling results: not active.** We do not sell shared benchmark results or anything else you share. Selling anonymised results would be a separate purpose that needs its own, explicit consent; the app does not ask for it, so nothing you share is sold. If we ever want to offer it, the app will ask you separately first and this policy will describe it before it applies; until you say yes to that, your results are used only for the leaderboard and to evaluate models and the harness.

Nie sprzedajemy tych danych i nie przekazujemy ich innym firmom do ich własnych celów. Pełne warunki udostępniania aplikacja pokazuje przed włączeniem udostępniania.

**Podstawa prawna.** Twoja zgoda (art. 6 ust. 1 lit. a RODO), wyrażona w aplikacji osobno dla każdego celu. Każda zmiana (włączenie, wyłączenie, dołączenie wcześniejszych czatów, włączenie lub wyłączenie zgody na trenowanie, ustawienie benchmarków, każdy udostępniony lub wycofany bieg benchmarku, usunięcie) jest zapisywana z czasem i wersją tych warunków, na Twoim komputerze i na naszym serwerze, żeby obie strony mogły wykazać, na co i kiedy była zgoda.

**Wycofanie zgody i usunięcie.** Udostępnianie, albo tylko zgodę na trenowanie modeli, możesz wyłączyć w każdej chwili w Ustawieniach, Udostępnianie danych. Bez udostępniania nic więcej nie jest wysyłane: czaty i wyniki benchmarków, które czekały jeszcze na wysłanie, razem z ich rozmowami, są usuwane z kolejki, a wysyłka trwająca w tej chwili zostaje przerwana (to, co serwer zdążył już odebrać, obejmuje usunięcie). Tak samo dzieje się po wyłączeniu udostępniania wyników benchmarków oraz dla biegu usuniętego w aplikacji, zanim jego wynik został wysłany; bez zgody na trenowanie żaden z Twoich udostępnionych czatów, także wcześniejszych, nie trafi do trenowania rozpoczętego później. Wycofanie zgody nie wpływa na zgodność z prawem przetwarzania sprzed wycofania. "Delete my shared data" kasuje wszystko, co jest zapisane pod Twoim identyfikatorem instalacji na naszym serwerze, przy najbliższym połączeniu, zwykle w ciągu kilku minut, a najpóźniej w ciągu 30 dni. Aplikacja pokazuje usunięcie jako wykonane dopiero wtedy, gdy nasz serwer je potwierdzi, z czasem serwera. Kopie danych serwera w naszych kopiach zapasowych nie są przez to kasowane: znikają, gdy te kopie zostaną zastąpione w zwykłej rotacji (przechowujemy 14 najnowszych codziennych kopii, więc usunięte dane znikają z nich zwykle w ciągu około dwóch tygodni), a do tego czasu nie są do niczego używane. Z modelu, który już został wytrenowany na Twoich czatach, nie da się ich usunąć; po usunięciu Twoje czaty nie są używane do dalszego trenowania.

**Jak długo.** Udostępnione czaty są usuwane automatycznie trzy lata po ich zapisaniu, a wcześniej, gdy je usuniesz albo wycofasz zgodę z prośbą o usunięcie; czaty, których już nie potrzebujemy, możemy usunąć wcześniej. Zapis zgód przechowujemy tak długo, jak musimy umieć wykazać zgodę, nie dłużej niż 6 lat od ostatniej zmiany: po "Delete my shared data" trafia on do osobnego archiwum razem z prośbą o usunięcie, a identyfikator instalacji i czas usunięcia są notowane w dzienniku usunięć przechowywanym przez ten sam okres, żebyśmy mogli wykazać, co i kiedy zostało usunięte. Techniczny dziennik zmian na serwerze odnotowuje, które rekordy zostały zapisane, zastąpione lub usunięte i kiedy, z identyfikatorem instalacji wyłącznie w postaci skrótu (hash) i bez żadnej treści; jest przechowywany przez ten sam okres. Te trzy zapisy przechowujemy po usunięciu danych na podstawie naszego prawnie uzasadnionego interesu w możliwości wykazania, że zgoda została udzielona i że dane zostały usunięte, oraz w ustaleniu, dochodzeniu lub obronie roszczeń (art. 6 ust. 1 lit. f RODO); 6 lat odpowiada ogólnemu terminowi przedawnienia roszczeń w prawie polskim. Po upływie tego okresu te zapisy są usuwane automatycznie: serwer sprawdza to codziennie. Jeśli kiedykolwiek trzeba będzie odtworzyć kopię zapasową, przed jej użyciem usuwamy z niej w ten sam sposób zapisy, których okres minął, oraz wszystko, co usunięto przez "Delete my shared data" po jej utworzeniu.
