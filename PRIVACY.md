# Privacy policy

_Last updated: 18 November 2026. The same text is on [localquench.com/privacy.html](https://localquench.com/privacy.html); Polish below the English._

Local Quench runs on your computer. Your code, chats and keys stay there. This page explains the few cases where personal data does leave it: visiting this website, the app's update checks and web search, and chats you decide to share with us.

## 2. This website

**Server logs.** When you open a page, the hosting provider's server records your IP address, the time, the page requested, the browser's user agent and the referring page. We use these logs only to keep the site running and secure (for example to find attacks or errors). Legal basis: our legitimate interest in a working, secure website (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). The hosting provider keeps the logs for a limited time under its own rules (14 days at the time of writing) and then deletes them.

**Cookies and similar.** This website sets no cookies and stores nothing in your browser (no tracking pixels, no third-party analytics, no advertising; our own counting is described below), with one exception: if your browser's language is Chinese, the English pages show a line "本站有中文版" ("this site has a Chinese version"), and if you close it, your browser remembers that in its local storage (one entry, `lq-zh-hint`), so the line stays closed. That entry is never sent to us, and you can clear it with your browser's site data. That is why it shows no cookie banner: there is nothing to consent to. Fonts are served from this server, not from Google or another font service. The model advisor runs entirely in your browser; what you type into it is not sent anywhere.

**Counting visits and downloads.** To know how the website is used, it keeps daily totals, and nothing else:

- how often each page is opened, and how many visits start on each page;
- where visits come from: only the domain of the site that linked here (for example github.com), or "direct";
- how long a page stays visible, in ranges (under 10 seconds, 10 to 30 seconds, and so on up to over 10 minutes);
- for each visit, the browser's language (for example "pl"), whether it is a phone or a computer, and the operating system, as the browser itself states them in every request;
- how often each download button is clicked, by system and version;
- how often the app checks for updates, by kind of install and version (section 3).

A small script on each page sends these counts to our website, where they are added to the day's totals. The counting stores nothing in your browser (no cookies, no local storage), and no IP address, identifier or browser fingerprint is stored or used, so a number cannot be linked to you or to your other visits. In your browser, the script only reads which site you came from and measures how long the page is visible. A browser that sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control sends nothing, and robots are not counted; you can also block the script or JavaScript. We use the totals only to understand how the website and the app are used. As far as any of it were personal data, the legal basis is our legitimate interest in knowing how our website is used (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). We keep the totals as long as they are useful. We also read how often GitHub has served each release file, a number GitHub shows for every release.

**E-mail.** If you write to us, we use your address and message to answer and keep the correspondence for as long as needed to handle the matter and possible claims (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

## 3. The Local Quench app

The app processes your projects, chats and settings on your computer. We do not receive them and are not the controller of that data. The app connects to the internet only in these cases:

- **Update checks.** The app asks whether a newer version exists: first our website (localquench.com, whose hosting logs are described in section 2), then GitHub (GitHub, Inc.), and downloads it from GitHub. The same check also fetches the public list of models for the model advisor. GitHub sees your IP address, as with any visit to github.com, under [its own privacy statement](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement). The check tells our website the app's version and kind of install (for example Windows or AppImage), so that we can count update checks per day; nothing that identifies your computer is sent. You can turn automatic checks off in Settings; then neither happens.
- **Price list for cloud models.** If you have set up a cloud provider, the app fetches current prices from OpenRouter (OpenRouter, Inc., United States) to show what a chat would cost there. With local models only, it does not.
- **Web search and reading web pages,** only when the agent uses those tools. Searches go to DuckDuckGo, pages are fetched directly from the site in question. Turn the tools off in Settings if you do not want this.
- **Cloud models you connect yourself** (for example Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, OpenRouter, NVIDIA). Your prompts and code then go to that provider under your own account and its terms. We are not part of that relationship and see nothing of it.
- **Phone access** works in one of two ways you choose. Over Tailscale between your own devices: no server of ours is involved, and Tailscale's own privacy policy covers its coordination service. Or directly (WebRTC): the phone and the computer connect to each other over the internet, encrypted end to end. To find each other they leave a few short messages in a mailbox on our server; these are encrypted with a key only your two devices have, so we cannot read them, and they are deleted when picked up or after 5 minutes at most. To keep the mailbox from being flooded, the server counts requests per address for a few minutes, using only a scrambled form of the IP address that changes daily; nothing is logged. To learn their public address, the devices ask public STUN servers (by default Google's and Cloudflare's), which see the IP address, as for any internet connection. Your chats, code and files never pass through our server.
- **Agent bridge (optional, off by default).** If you turn on the agent bridge in Local Quench and connect another program on your computer to it, such as Claude Code, Codex or Claude Desktop, that program can start tasks in Local Quench. The text of each task, its result, the list of changed files and, if that program asks for them, the changes made to those files and the conversation of that task go to that program, which may send them to its provider (for example Anthropic or OpenAI). What that program and its provider do with them is governed by their terms, not by this policy, and we are not responsible for it. Inside Local Quench such a task is an ordinary conversation: if you have turned on sharing, it is shared with us like any other conversation, under the consents you gave. It is marked with the name of the program that started it, and since its instructions were written by a cloud model, it is never used to train models, like a chat with a cloud model. If you do not want this, leave the bridge off.
- **Chat sharing,** only if you turn it on. See the next section.

GitHub, OpenRouter, DuckDuckGo, Google and Cloudflare (STUN) are companies based in the United States and see your IP address under their own policies, as with any connection to them. Where such a company is certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, that is the basis for the transfer; otherwise the standard contractual clauses in its terms.

## 4. Sharing chats to improve Local Quench

**What.** If you turn sharing on, the app sends us your chats with the agent from that moment on (and earlier ones only if you also tick "Also chats from before I turned it on"): your messages, the model's replies, the tool calls and their output, the model name, harness settings and timings. Before anything leaves your computer, the app removes API keys, tokens, passwords, e-mail addresses, your home folder and the beginning of every other absolute path, and you can see the original next to the redacted version in Settings. The redaction is pattern matching and cannot recognise everything: a key split in two or encoded, names, company details or personal data inside your code may remain. Do not turn sharing on for projects with personal data of other people or confidential code.

**Identity.** Chats are stored under a random install identifier created by the app. We do not ask for your name or e-mail, and we use your IP address only to deliver the upload and, to keep the server from being flooded, to count requests per address for a few minutes in a scrambled form that changes daily; not to identify you. Because a chat can still contain personal data, we treat shared chats as personal data.

**Purpose.** Two purposes, each with its own consent. (a) Improving Local Quench: analysing where models and the harness fail, evaluating changes and building test tasks. (b) Training and evaluating machine learning models, only if you also tick "Also allow training models with my shared chats". Models trained this way may be published (for example as downloadable weights); the chats themselves are not. Without consent (b) your chats are never used to train a model. Chats with a cloud model (for example Claude, GPT or Gemini) are never used to train models, even with consent (b): their providers' terms do not allow it; the app marks them. We do not sell the data and do not give it to other companies for their own purposes. The full sharing terms are shown in the app before you can turn sharing on.

**Legal basis.** Your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR), given in the app separately for each purpose. Each change (on, off, earlier chats included or not, training on or off, the benchmark setting, each benchmark run you share or withdraw, deletion) is recorded with its time and the version of these terms, on your computer and on our server, so both sides can show what was allowed when.

**Withdrawal and deletion.** You can turn sharing off, or only the permission to train models, at any time in Settings, Data sharing. Without sharing, nothing more is sent: chats and benchmark results that were still waiting to be sent, with their conversations, are dropped, and an upload that is under way at that moment is stopped (what the server had already received by then is covered by deletion). The same holds when you turn off sharing of benchmark results, and for a run you delete in the app before its result was sent; without the training permission, none of your shared chats, earlier ones included, is used in any training that starts afterwards. Withdrawal does not affect what was done lawfully before it. "Delete my shared data" removes everything stored under your install identifier on our server with the next connection, normally within minutes, and in any case within 30 days. The app shows the deletion as done only once our server has confirmed it, with the server's time. Copies of the server's data in our backups are not erased by this: they disappear when those backups are replaced in their normal rotation (the newest 14 daily copies are kept, so deleted data normally leaves them within about two weeks), and are not used for anything else in the meantime. A model that has already been trained on your chats cannot have them removed from it; after deletion your chats are not used for any further training.

**How long.** Shared chats are deleted automatically three years after they were stored, and earlier when you delete them or withdraw consent and ask for deletion; we may delete chats we no longer need before that. The consent record is kept for as long as we need to prove consent, at most 6 years after the last change: after "Delete my shared data" it is moved to a separate archive together with the deletion request, and the install identifier and time of the deletion are noted in a deletion log kept for the same period, so we can show what was deleted and when. A technical change log on the server notes which records were stored, replaced or deleted and when, with your install identifier only in hashed form and without any content; it is kept for the same period. We keep these three records after a deletion on the basis of our legitimate interest in being able to show that consent was given and that data was deleted, and in establishing, exercising or defending legal claims (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR); the 6 years match the general limitation period for claims under Polish law. Once that period has passed, these records are deleted automatically: the server checks for them every day. If a backup ever has to be restored, before it is used we delete from it in the same way the records whose period has passed and everything removed with "Delete my shared data" after the backup was made.

**Where.** On a server we rent from Hetzner Online GmbH (Industriestr. 25, 91710 Gunzenhausen, Germany) in the European Union, which processes the data only on our instructions under a data processing agreement. We do not transfer shared chats outside the European Economic Area. If that changes for training on rented computers, it will happen only under the safeguards of Chapter V GDPR, and this policy will say so before it applies.

### Benchmark results

Separately from chats, you can add a QuenchBench result to the public leaderboard. The app asks after every finished run and shows exactly what would be sent: the model and its quantization, the model server and its settings (engine and version, KV cache type, sampling settings), your processor, graphics card, memory, the benchmark settings (including any harness switches and the label you gave the run), the app version, when the run started and finished, and the per-task results (pass or fail, time, tokens, speed). No code, chats, file paths or commands. Each result is sent only after you click Share for that run. **Conversations of a run.** In the same dialog you can also tick "Also send the agent's conversations from this run" (if chat sharing is on, they always go with a result you share, since you have already allowed sharing chats): then the run's chats go with the result, that is the task text, the model's replies and the commands it ran on our task files, scrubbed like any shared chat (keys, tokens and your home folder removed). QuenchBench tasks are ours, so these conversations contain nothing from your projects. They are stored with the result under your install identifier, used to improve the harness, used to train and evaluate models only if you also gave the separate permission "Also allow training models with my shared chats" (and never when the model was a cloud API; ticking this box alone is not that permission), never sold, and deleted together with the result, at the latest three years after they were stored. A result and its conversations that were still waiting to be sent are not sent when you turn sharing or benchmark sharing off, or delete the run in the app. Legal basis: your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR). Results are stored under your install's random id, published on the leaderboard grouped by model, quantization, KV cache type, graphics card and task selection (a group can be one computer's results), with memory shown as its size and kind (for example 16 GB DDR5), never the exact amount; a run on a cloud API is marked as such and shows no graphics card, and removed with "Delete my shared data".

### Performance measurements

Separately from chats and benchmark results, the app can share anonymous measurements of how models load and run on your computer. This is off by default and has its own switch, its own terms (shown in the app before you can turn it on) and its own consent record, in Settings, Data sharing, "Share performance measurements (anonymous)". "Preview" shows exactly what the next upload contains.

**What.** One record per model load, only for loads made after you turned it on: your graphics card and processor (product names as your system reports them), video memory and RAM (rounded), the graphics driver's major version, the llama.cpp build (family, version, backend), the model (its public Hugging Face repository and file name when known; otherwise only a hash of the file name together with its architecture, quantization, size and parameter count), the load settings (context size, layers on the GPU, KV cache type, batch size, threads, speculative decoding mode), the memory the app estimated and the engine used, how long loading took, whether it worked and, if not, the classified reason (for example out of memory) and exit code, and prompt and generation speed and speculative acceptance rate at different context depths. Dates are cut to the day. **Never sent:** chats, prompts, code, file or folder names, project names, your user name or computer name, e-mail, or the app's chat-sharing identifier.

**Identity.** The records are stored under a random identifier made only for this purpose. It is not the identifier used for chat sharing and is stored nowhere next to it, so we cannot join the two datasets. The data is pseudonymous, not anonymous in the legal sense: a rare combination of hardware could in principle be recognised, so we treat it as personal data. We use your IP address only to deliver the upload and, to keep the server from being flooded, to count requests per address for a few minutes in a scrambled form that changes daily; it is not stored with the data.

**Purpose and legal basis.** Finding which settings load and run well on which hardware, giving better default settings and showing how local models perform (we may publish figures combined from many users, such as the median speed of a model on a graphics card, never an individual's records). Legal basis: your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR), which you can withdraw at any time. Each change is recorded with its time and the version of the terms on your computer and on our server. We do not sell performance measurements and do not give them to other companies for their own purposes. **How we publish statistics.** A figure we publish or give out is calculated from at least 10 different random identifiers; hardware that fewer than 10 identifiers share is merged into a coarser class (for example a graphics card model becomes its memory size class) or left out, so a rare configuration is never shown on its own.

**Withdrawal and deletion.** Turning it off in Settings stops everything at once and drops what was waiting to be sent. "Delete my measurements" removes the local measurements and everything stored on our server under your random identifier (one folder) with the next connection and in any case within 30 days, and the app then makes a new random identifier. The app shows the deletion as done only once the server has confirmed it. As for chats, copies in our backups disappear when those backups are replaced (about two weeks), and the consent record and the deletion log entry (the identifier and the time) are kept for at most 6 years (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, to show consent and deletion).

**How long and where.** Stored measurements are deleted automatically three years after they were stored, or earlier when you delete them. They are stored on the same Hetzner Online GmbH server in Germany, in the European Union, as described above, under the same data processing agreement; there are no other recipients.

### Helper check results

This is off by default and has its own switch, its own terms (shown in the app before you can turn it on) and its own consent record, in Settings, Data sharing, "Share helper check results (anonymous)". "Preview" shows exactly what is queued for the next upload and, before anything is queued, how your last check would look.

**What.** One record for each helper a check measured with a clear result (a check without one is not sent), only for checks you start after you turned it on: the helper's name, its value with and without, the verdict (helps, hurts, no difference, or cheaper / costlier when the same tasks passed with clearly fewer / more tokens and time), for each side how many of the tasks passed and the average steps, tokens and seconds, the model (its public Hugging Face repository and file name when known; otherwise only a hash of the file name) with its quantization, the harness tier and tool set it had, whether thinking was on, the app version, your graphics card (product name as your system reports it, video memory rounded, backend), the llama.cpp build (family, version) and the day. **Never sent:** the text or files of the test tasks, code, chats, prompts, file or folder names, project names, your user name or computer name, e-mail, or the app's chat-sharing or performance identifiers.

**Identity.** The records are stored under a random identifier made only for this purpose. It is neither the identifier used for chat sharing nor the one used for performance measurements and is stored nowhere next to them, so we cannot join the datasets. The data is pseudonymous, not anonymous in the legal sense, and we treat it as personal data. We use your IP address only to deliver the upload and, to keep the server from being flooded, to count requests per address for a few minutes in a scrambled form that changes daily; it is not stored with the data.

**Purpose and legal basis.** Finding which helpers help which models, giving better default settings and showing other users how a helper did for their model: the app downloads the published figures (for example "helped in 70% of 140 runs") and shows them next to your own result, and may suggest a helper for a model you have not checked. The app applies nothing until you click. Legal basis: your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR), which you can withdraw at any time. Each change is recorded with its time and the version of the terms on your computer and on our server. We do not sell these results and do not give them to other companies for their own purposes. **How we publish statistics.** A figure we publish is calculated from at least 10 different random identifiers for the same model and helper (the number of runs and the share where it helped, hurt, made no difference, was cheaper or was costlier); anything with fewer is not published. One identifier counts with at most its five most recent runs per model and helper.

**Withdrawal and deletion.** Turning it off in Settings stops everything at once and drops what was waiting to be sent. "Delete my results" removes everything stored on our server under your random identifier (one folder) with the next connection and in any case within 30 days, and the app then makes a new random identifier; the results on your computer stay. The published figures are made again without your results within minutes. As for chats, copies in our backups disappear when those backups are replaced (about two weeks), and the consent record and the deletion log entry (the identifier and the time) are kept for at most 6 years (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, to show consent and deletion).

**How long and where.** Stored results are deleted automatically three years after they were stored, or earlier when you delete them. They are stored on the same Hetzner Online GmbH server in Germany, in the European Union, as described above, under the same data processing agreement; there are no other recipients.

### "First working chat" count

The app asks once, after your first chat with a local model has worked, whether it may tell us that. This is off unless you say yes, and you can change it any time in Settings, Data sharing. If you say yes, the app sends one message the first time a chat turn with a local model finishes without an error: **the app version, the kind of system (windows, macos or linux) and a random identifier** made for this message alone. The app shows you exactly this message before you answer. **Never sent:** anything from the chat, files, names, paths, hardware or settings. The identifier is not the one used for chat sharing, performance measurements or helper check results, so we cannot join them. After the message is counted, the app never sends it again; if your connection fails, it tries again at the next finished chat.

**Purpose and legal basis.** Counting how many people get from the download to a working chat, to see whether installation and first start work. Legal basis: your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR). We use your IP address only to deliver the message and to count requests per address for a few minutes in a scrambled form that changes daily; it is not stored with it. Turning it off before the first working chat means nothing is ever sent. A message that was already counted can be removed on request, if you send us the identifier shown in Settings, Data sharing. It is kept as long as the count is useful and stored on the same Hetzner Online GmbH server in Germany, in the European Union; there are no other recipients.

Udostępnianie jest przeznaczone dla osób dorosłych; jeśli masz mniej niż 16 lat, nie włączaj go bez zgody rodzica.

**Użytkownicy spoza UE.** Działamy z Polski, a dane przechowujemy w Niemczech, więc przepisy UE mają do nich zastosowanie niezależnie od tego, gdzie mieszkasz. *Stany Zjednoczone, w tym Kalifornia (CCPA/CPRA):* nie sprzedajemy Twoich danych osobowych ani nie udostępniamy ich do reklamy behawioralnej między kontekstami, nie używamy danych wrażliwych do wnioskowania o Twoich cechach, a możesz zapytać, jakie dane o Tobie mamy, i zażądać ich usunięcia, pod podanym adresem lub w aplikacji, bez gorszego traktowania z tego powodu. *Chiny (PIPL):* udostępnianie jest dobrowolne i oparte na Twojej zgodzie, którą możesz wycofać w dowolnym momencie; włączając udostępnianie w aplikacji, zgadzasz się, że dane są przekazywane do Unii Europejskiej (Niemcy), tam przechowywane i przetwarzane przez nas w Polsce, o czym informuje regulamin udostępniania, zanim wyrazisz zgodę; masz dostęp do danych, możesz je skopiować, sprostować i usunąć, jak opisano w punkcie 5. Nie twierdzimy, że ta polityka spełnia każde inne prawo krajowe; napisz do nas, jeśli uważasz, że przepis, który Cię dotyczy, nie jest spełniony.

O zmianach tej polityki poinformujemy na tej stronie, a w sprawie udostępniania czatów także w aplikacji, zanim zaczną obowiązywać. Zmiana celu wykorzystania udostępnionych czatów wymaga Twojej nowej zgody.
